Findings

Ordered by severity, with explicit local evidence and external benchmark context.

Critical

Transparency is not uniform across the portfolio

Only the Finerenone family currently exposes the full reviewer stack in one place: visible search strings, eligibility workflow, evidence excerpts, demographics, RoB, GRADE, PRISMA, search-history logging, and in-browser WebR.

High

Most topic pages remain constrained-source reviews, not full publication-level systematic reviews

Finerenone explicitly states that eligibility and endpoint fit are verified against CT.gov plus PubMed/OpenAlex abstracts with no full-text extraction, and several pages warn readers to verify registry-derived values against published manuscripts before finalizing.

Moderate

ESC ACS Studio is topic-rich but transparency-light

ESC ACS exposes raw records, ROB2, and GRADE, but it does not visibly expose PRISMA, demographics, evidence excerpts, or WebR in the main entry point. It looks more like a synthesis studio than a publication-ready living review.

The base app points only to CT.gov as a data source, while the engine centers charts and RoB without a visible acquisition, screening, evidence-ledger, or validation layer. They should not be presented as equivalent to the stronger disease-specific pages.

Persona Review

What each reviewer type would care about most.

Methods Reviewer

Finerenone and Watchman/Amulet are the only current builds that read like explicit review protocols with visible search logic and PRISMA-ready reporting. TEER and PFA are close but remain slimmer and more manual.

Evidence Provenance Reviewer

The main portfolio risk is constrained-source extraction. Several apps correctly admit that values are registry-derived or abstract-linked and should be checked against publications, but that also means the current evidence layer is not yet self-sufficient.

Statistics Reviewer

Only Finerenone currently gives a browser-executable R check. TEER and PFA expose R scripts but still require trust that the user ran them. ESC ACS and the generic layers need a reviewer mode before they are publication-grade.

Clinical Reviewer

Watchman/Amulet is strong on clinically relevant protocol caveats, especially indirectness around Watchman 2.5 versus FLX. TEER and PFA provide useful landmarks, but full publication reconciliation still sits outside the app.

UX Reviewer

The best pages expose audit logs, extraction toggles, and SoF tables. The weak pages hide too much of the review process behind synthesis outputs, which is the wrong trade-off for skeptical readers and peer reviewers.

Published-Meta Benchmarks

PubMed-only benchmark search used to compare local scope with conventional published meta-analyses.

Finerenone

Recent published finerenone meta-analyses searched PubMed plus Embase/CENTRAL/ClinicalTrials.gov and pooled 8 to 9 randomized trials with 21,200 to 21,731 participants.

Local build strength: transparent protocol, source excerpts, PRISMA, GRADE, WebR. Local gap: it openly remains a constrained-source workflow with no full-text extraction.

PubMed 41166566 PubMed 39911073 PubMed 36571667
Tricuspid TEER

Published TEER meta-analyses search PubMed/Scopus/Cochrane or PubMed/Embase/Cochrane and frame the question as comparative effectiveness or device-specific outcomes.

Local build strength: PRISMA, GRADE, demographics, exportable R script. Local gap: no browser-side WebR and visible verification warnings against primary publications.

PubMed 40810290 PubMed 37473817
PFA AF

Recent published meta-analyses search PubMed plus Embase/Cochrane and increasingly use Bayesian random-effects models, IPD reconstruction from Kaplan-Meier curves, and explicit observational-versus-RCT contrasts.

Local build strength: PRISMA, GRADE, audit log, R validation export. Local gap: current acquisition is CT.gov plus PubMed cross-check, not the broader full review stack.

PubMed 41759868 PubMed 41578543 PubMed 39416254
Watchman vs Amulet

Published device-comparison meta-analyses search PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane and explicitly separate legacy WATCHMAN from Watchman FLX-era evidence.

Local build strength: strongest protocol caveats, indirectness handling, source citations, and PRISMA/GRADE display. Local gap: missing browser-side WebR and one acquisition counter bug.

PubMed 41397490 PubMed 38948665
ESC ACS Studio

No single like-for-like published comparator was assessed, because the project is a multi-topic ACS synthesis studio rather than one fixed clinical question. The right benchmark here is topic-specific: each selected ACS topic should surface prior meta-analysis trial IDs, search strings, PRISMA, and a reviewer validation mode.

Current local evidence already hints at this with a dedicated "Prior meta-analysis trial IDs" input, but the reviewer layer is not yet built through.

Priority Backlog

What to do next if the goal is reviewer-proof transparency across the whole portfolio.

1

Lift the Finerenone reviewer layer into TEER, PFA, Watchman, and ESC ACS: visible search strings, search-history log, evidence ledger, manuscript-verification flags, and browser-side WebR.

2

Add per-record include and exclude reasons that remain visible after selection, not just PRISMA counts. Reviewers should be able to audit each excluded abstract from inside the page.

3

Add a "published-meta comparator" panel to each disease-specific review with direct links, search dates, database scope, trial overlap, and effect-size deltas versus the local synthesis.

4

Fix provenance-display bugs before claiming full transparency, starting with the Watchman OpenAlex counter wiring.

5

Require every app to state whether it is CT.gov-only, abstract-linked, or full-text reconciled. Reviewers should never have to infer the evidence ceiling from scattered notes.
